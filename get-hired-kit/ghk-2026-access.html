Prompt
Scam check: walk away if you see any of these
  • They ask you to pay for anything: training, software, equipment, a "registration fee."
  • They send you a check or money to buy equipment and "send back the rest."
  • The whole interview runs over text only (Telegram, WhatsApp, Signal), with no call and no company you can find.
  • They ask for your bank login, ID scan or tax number before a written offer.
  • The pay is far above the market for the work, with "no experience needed."
  • The company name, website or LinkedIn doesn't exist or was created last week.
  • They want you to receive packages at home and re-ship them.

A real client will never ask you to pay to work for them.

Step 2

Write the application

The person hiring reads the first two lines of 50 applications, on their phone. Yours must name the main task, the tools and the time zone before anything else. Skills lists, your story and your rate come later, or not at all.

No clients yet? Make your proof in an hour

You don't need a past client to have proof. You need a finished example. Pick the one that matches this job, do it, and use it as your proof line.

  • Inbox job: set up a label and filter system for a practice inbox of 1,000+ emails. Screenshot before and after.
  • Social media job: write and schedule 5 sample posts for a real local business as a practice project.
  • Data entry job: clean a 500-row lead list. Remove duplicates, standardise formatting, share before and after.
  • Scheduling job: build a booking system in Calendly for a mock coaching business, with buffers and reminders.

Always say it was a practice project. One honest hour of work beats a paragraph about how fast you learn.

Words that get applications skipped

"Dear Sir/Madam." "I am writing to apply for." "Hardworking, dedicated and detail-oriented." "I can do any task." "Kindly." "I have gone through your job post." "I am the perfect candidate."

Step 3

The interview

Clients are told to ask "tell me about a time" questions and to watch for two things: specific answers and good questions back. Answer each one as situation, what you did, result, in under a minute. Never claim experience you don't have. Practice projects count if you say they were practice.

Your questions for them (always ask two)
  • "What would make you say this hire was a success after 30 days?"
  • "What's the task you most want off your plate first?"
  • "How do you prefer updates: email, Slack, or a quick call?"
  • "Did someone do this work before, and what would you like done differently?"
Step 4

The paid test task

Most clients now hire after a short paid trial. They aren't only checking the result. They're checking whether you followed instructions, asked good questions, and made it easy to review.

Rules for every test task
  1. Read the instructions twice and do exactly what was asked. Extras come after, marked as optional.
  2. Ask your questions once, all together, before you start.
  3. Hit the deadline, or tell them before it passes.
  4. Send it back with a short cover note: what you did, any assumption, time taken, one suggestion.
  5. Make it easy to check. Clear file names, and links that open without requesting access.
