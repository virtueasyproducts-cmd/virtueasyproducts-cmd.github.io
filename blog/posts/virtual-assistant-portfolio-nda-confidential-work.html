How to Show Your VA Work When You Cannot Share Client Details (Redacted Samples, Anonymized Results and Mock Projects)

2 November 2026 · Virtueasy · Portfolio & Proof · 7 min read

You have done real work. Good work. You cut a client's inbox backlog in half, rebuilt their CRM contacts from a mess into something usable, and kept their calendar running for three months without a missed meeting. And your portfolio still looks blank.

This is the most common stuck point for VAs with one to three clients. The work happened. You just cannot show it, because it lives inside someone else's private accounts, and your contract says you keep it there.

That is not a dead end. It is a process problem, and process problems have solutions. Here is how to show proof without breaking trust.

Step One: Read Your Actual Contract

Before you redact a screenshot or write up a result, open the agreement you signed with that client and read the confidentiality section. Every contract is different. Some prohibit sharing any client information in any form. Some allow you to reference the work in general terms as long as no identifying details appear. A few explicitly allow portfolio use with written approval.

This post is not legal advice. What is in your contract governs what you can do. If the language is unclear, ask the client directly or check with a professional. Do not assume that blurring a name is enough if your contract says something stricter.

What you can always do is ask for permission. One short message is all it takes:

"I'm building out my portfolio and wanted to ask if you'd be comfortable with me sharing a brief anonymized version of the work I did for you. No names, no specifics, just the type of task and the outcome. Let me know either way."

Some clients will say yes. Some will say no. Either answer costs you nothing and keeps the relationship clean.

What Is Safe to Show and What Is Not

The rule is simple, even if applying it takes judgment. You are showing your skill, not their business.

Safe to show: the type of task, the system or tool you used, the outcome in general terms, your process, and anything you built on dummy or fictional data.

Never show: a client's name or logo without written permission, their customers' names or contact details, anything from inside their CRM or email, financial figures tied to a specific business, login credentials, internal documents, or any data that could let someone identify who the client is.

If you are not sure whether something is identifying, assume it is and remove it.

How to Redact a Screenshot Properly

A blurred rectangle is not enough. Text under a blur can sometimes be recovered, and a partly visible email address or company name gives away more than you think.

The right way to redact is to remove the sensitive content entirely and replace it with a label. Use a solid color block, not a blur, and type a plain label over it: [Client Name], [Email Address], [Company], [Dollar Amount]. Do this in an image editor and flatten it before you export, not as a layer sitting on top of the finished image. When you are done, zoom in and check every line. If you can read anything you should not be sharing, fix it before the file leaves your computer.

What you end up with is a screenshot that shows the tool, the workflow and your thinking, without exposing the person it belongs to.

How to Write an Anonymized Result

Specific outcomes are the most persuasive proof you have. You do not need a name attached to make them believable.

Write the result with enough context to be meaningful, and strip everything that points to the client. Instead of "I helped [Company Name] reduce their email response time," write "a three person bookkeeping firm cut inbox response time from two days to same day." The industry gives it context. The size gives it scale. The result speaks for itself.

You can also describe the type of work with no result at all: "managed social media scheduling for a product-based e-commerce brand" tells a prospective client what you can do without revealing who you did it for.

How to Build a Mock Project That Shows the Real Skill

If your actual client work is entirely off limits, build an example that shows the same skill on fictional data. This is not padding your portfolio. It is showing that you can do the thing, with a clearly labeled example you built yourself.

Pick a realistic scenario: a fictional small business in an industry you know, and a task you have handled before. Build it out the way you would for a real client. Set up the spreadsheet, write the SOP, design the email sequence, organize the project board. Then screenshot it, export it, and label it clearly as a mock project.

The label matters. Put "Mock project: created for portfolio purposes" at the top of the document or image. No reasonable client will hold a practice example against you. They will hold it against you if they later find out a sample was presented as real work when it was not.

Label Everything Honestly

Three labels cover every situation:

  • Redacted sample: real work with identifying information removed.
  • Anonymized result: a real outcome with the client details left out.
  • Mock project: a demonstration you built yourself.

Use them. A prospect who understands what they are looking at trusts what they see. One who later finds out a "client result" was an unlabeled practice exercise will not hire you, and may tell others.

Keep a Running Proof Folder as You Work

The hardest part of building a portfolio is trying to reconstruct proof six months after a project ended. Details fade, screenshots are gone, and you may not remember the exact numbers anymore.

The easier approach is to capture proof as you go. When you finish a task or hit a result worth noting, take a screenshot and drop it in a folder labeled with the date and a short description. Write down the outcome in plain language while it is fresh. Note what can be anonymized and what cannot. Do this for every client, every week, even if you are not building your portfolio yet.

When you sit down to put a case study or portfolio sample together, it takes ten minutes instead of an afternoon of trying to remember.

Your Portfolio Does Not Have to Wait

You have options. Redacted screenshots show the real tool and the real work. Anonymized results show the real outcome. Mock projects show the real skill. All three are honest, and all three can go in your portfolio today without a single client detail attached.

Start with the work you have done in the last ninety days. Write down three results in the anonymized format. Pick one task you can rebuild on fictional data. Label everything clearly. Put it somewhere a prospective client can see it.

Your work is not invisible. It just needed a format that protects everyone involved.
