How to Follow Up With a VA Prospect Who Went Quiet

16 October 2026 · Virtueasy · Follow-Up · 6 min read

They did not reply. That does not mean no. Here is how to follow up like a pro and actually land the client.

You sent the DM. Maybe you had a great discovery call. You sent the proposal. And then nothing. No reply, no thank you, not even a polite decline. Just silence.

If you are a new virtual assistant, that silence probably feels like rejection. It is tempting to either fire off a frantic "just checking in!" message or give up entirely and tell yourself they were not serious anyway. Most new VAs do one or the other. And both options cost them the client.

Here is what is actually happening most of the time: your prospect is busy. Not indifferent, not unimpressed, busy. They opened your message between meetings, meant to reply, and forgot. Your proposal is sitting in a tab they plan to get back to. Plenty of first clients are won on the second, third, or even fourth touchpoint. The VAs who land those clients are the ones who follow up with calm confidence, not nervous nudges.

This post is your plan for doing exactly that.

Why Silence Usually Means Busy, Not No

Business owners who hire virtual assistants are almost always stretched thin. That is why they are looking for help in the first place. When a prospect goes quiet, they are rarely making a deliberate decision to ignore you. More often, your message arrived at the wrong moment, or they are still figuring out their own budget, scope, or timeline. Silence is ambiguity, not a closed door. Treat it that way and your follow-up will come across as genuinely helpful rather than desperate.

Treat Follow-Up as a Planned Sequence, Not a One-Off Message

The moment you send a proposal or a DM, decide in advance that you will follow up three times if needed. Write it down. This removes the emotional guesswork. You are not chasing anyone. You are executing a professional sequence with clear purpose, clear spacing, and a clear ending point.

Here is a realistic cadence for three follow-ups, whether your last contact was a DM, a discovery call, or a proposal.

Follow-Up One: Add Something Useful (3 to 5 Days After Initial Contact)

Wait at least three business days, then send a short message that adds something instead of asking for a status update. Reference a specific detail from your conversation or something you noticed about their business. Offer a relevant thought, a small observation, or a quick resource.

Sample message: "Hi [Name], I wanted to circle back after our conversation. I was thinking about the scheduling challenge you mentioned and realized that is something I handle often for clients. I put together a quick overview of how I approach it, and I am happy to share it if it would be useful. Either way, I am still interested in supporting your work if the timing is right."

This message is useful. It reminds them of a real problem they mentioned. It gives them a reason to reply that feels low-pressure.

Follow-Up Two: Ask a Clear Yes or No Question (7 to 10 Days Later)

If you have still not heard back, wait another week to ten days and send a second message. Keep it brief. The goal here is to make replying as easy as possible by asking something with a short answer.

Sample message: "Hi [Name], just wanted to check in one more time. Are you still exploring options for VA support, or has your situation changed? Either answer is completely fine. I just want to make sure your message has not fallen off my radar."

A simple yes or no question removes the friction of composing a full reply. It also signals that you respect their time and are not going to make a non-response awkward.

Follow-Up Three: The Close-the-Loop Message (10 to 14 Days Later)

This is the message new VAs almost never send, and it is often the one that gets the fastest reply. The close-the-loop message signals that you are wrapping up your outreach, with no pressure and no hard sell. It works because it creates a natural moment for the prospect to respond before the conversation officially closes.

Sample message: "Hi [Name], I am going to stop following up after this so I do not fill your inbox. If the timing is ever right for support with [specific task or challenge], I would genuinely love to help. Feel free to reach back out anytime. Wishing you a great [week/month/quarter]."

That is it. No ask. No desperation. Just a professional, warm close. Many prospects reply to this one simply because it removes all pressure.

When to Stop and What to Do Instead

After three follow-ups with no response, stop active outreach. Move the prospect to a "someday" list: a simple note or spreadsheet row with their name, how you connected, and when you last reached out. Check back in four to six months with a single no-pressure message referencing something new, like a service you have added, a result you have achieved for another client, or something relevant to their business.

This habit keeps the door open without making anyone feel followed. And it keeps you from mentally writing off leads who are genuinely just not ready yet.

What Each Follow-Up Must Do

Before you send any follow-up message, ask yourself whether it does at least one of these three things: adds a relevant idea, makes a small useful observation about their business, or asks a question that is easy to answer. If a message does none of those things, it is just "checking in," and checking in without purpose rarely moves anyone forward.

Your One-Line Tracking Habit

Keep a simple list. It can be a notes app, a spreadsheet, a sticky note, anything. Write the prospect's name, how you connected, what you sent, and when. Then write the date of your next planned follow-up. That is it. Fifteen seconds of admin means nothing falls through and no follow-up ever comes from a place of panic.

The Bigger Picture

Client relationship management starts before someone is even a client. How you show up during the pre-sale silence says something about how you will show up when they are paying you. Follow-up done well is not a sign of desperation. It is a demonstration of organization, professionalism, and genuine interest in helping someone solve a real problem.

Send the follow-up. The next client you land may be someone who needed a second message to finally say yes.
